* Conference version (36d156218)

*If* you want exploration versus exploitation to depend on operational pressure (deadlines, repeated failures, instability), not on randomness alone. *However* without an explicit dial the agent either thrashes or tunnels, and there is nothing to turn. *Then* maintain a /commitment temperature/ governing how decisively the best-scoring candidate wins: turned down, the system commits to its front-runner; turned up, it keeps the field open --- and couple that dial to diagnostic signals (recent regressions, uncertainty in the belief state, contradiction rate, time pressure). *Because* a single commitment dial allows systematic control of stochasticity without changing the scoring model at all. /Invariant to preserve:/ exploration is a dial, not a random number generator buried in the selector.

* Current version (d721e8736)

*If* you want exploration versus exploitation to depend on operational pressure (deadlines, repeated failures, instability), not on randomness alone. *However* without an explicit dial the agent either thrashes or tunnels, and there is nothing to turn. *Then* maintain a /commitment temperature/ governing how decisively the best-scoring candidate wins: turned down, the system commits to its front-runner; turned up, it keeps the field open --- and couple that dial to diagnostic signals (recent regressions, uncertainty in the belief state, contradiction rate, time pressure). *Because* a single commitment dial allows systematic control of stochasticity without changing the scoring model at all. /Invariant to preserve:/ exploration is a dial, not a random number generator buried in the selector. /Status in this implementation:/ the dial is connected only under the declared full-score selection law. computes the temperature and the habit-adjusted score vector. When =:full-score-posterior= is requested and \(F_\pi\) entered this tick, it selects the maximum full score and records =:habit-authority :live-in-selection-score=; otherwise it takes the head of R5's controller-score ordering and records =:habit-authority :counterfactual-only=. The decision record names the requested and applied laws and records a refusal when missing \(F_\pi\) prevents the requested law from running. The dial is therefore live on one declared path and explicitly counterfactual on the fallback path, rather than silently changing meanings between them.

Two dials exist and are kept apart. selection_gain.clj is the pattern's dial: a bounded scalar (floor 0.5, cap 2.0) that divides the spread-adaptive softmax temperature in policy.clj, learned from the chosen policies' realised-versus-expected performance; its docstring states plainly that it is an engineering commitment control, not Friston's policy precision. policy_precision.clj separately implements Friston's policy precision gamma by within-tick fixed-point iteration. On the live route of the 10-17/18 campaigns, though, the outer selector takes a deterministic pairwise minimum of task-state G (the limit of infinite precision), so neither dial changes which target is picked there; the inner reading policy runs under the operator-declared beta = 1. So the dial exists and turns, but the live outer decision does not pass through it.

The only learning signal is the chosen policy's signed, scale-free performance ratio (did its realised G beat its expected G). None of the four diagnostic signals named here feeds the dial. The current text keeps the same sentence, but its worknote is accurate: a tripwire coupling is ruled (factor one-half while a genuine trip is open) but is OUTSTANDING, not wired into any gain mode. Learning precision from realised outcomes is close to how active inference updates policy precision, so the code is AIF-valid and, by the revision policy, the main text should say what the dial actually learns from.
