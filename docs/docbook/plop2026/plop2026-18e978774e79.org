* Conference version (36d156218)

*If* a fast tactical loop acts inside a slower strategic loop. *However* merely giving both levels a scalar score does not create a hierarchy. *Then* make the coupling explicit: strategic selection fixes the tactical target; witnessed tactical outcomes update the next strategic calibration state; and longer policies use a /named/ temporal discount of their own rather than overloading the commitment dial. *Because* hierarchy is a relationship between levels and timescales, not a second use of the same score. /Invariant to preserve:/ each level has its own quantities, and no quantity does duty at two levels at once. /Current evidence:/ the opt-in two-tick campaign records both levels separately, rejects unwitnessed learning, folds a grounded fast outcome into a Beta update of the slow state, and uses that state to parameterise the next fast rollout. Campaign S supplied the live positive branch: Tick A's independently approved and grounded outcome advanced the relevant slow posterior from Beta(1,1) to Beta(2,1), changed the slow mode from exploration to consolidation, and parameterised Tick B under that new state. This is evidence for the explicit coupling, not for a general nested generative model.

This is evidence for the explicit coupling, not for a general nested generative model. /Work in progress:/ the Lean statement of R15 () reads the strategic target class from a distance to the current focus, where two targets are near when work on one changes what is expected to be observed about the other. No estimator of that distance exists yet. Consecutive tick records do form a panel of worked target against observed target: 52 records name a chosen target, giving 833 rows, but these cover only 6 observed targets, and observed state changes in 18 of them, 16 on a target other than the one worked. That is too little to compare against a similarity or edge-based seed. Nor can a change be credited to the chosen work, because any seat's commits between two records can alter an observation. Recording the commit range between consecutive tick records would supply that attribution without changing the record format; coverage grows only as the machine is run on more targets.

Campaign S's Beta(1,1) to Beta(2,1) belongs to the older opt-in two-tick route. The strategic belief then learns that the mission fails, when the evidence is that the machine failed. That is not a 'witnessed tactical outcome' in the pattern's sense: in the observation model, machine failure and task outcome are different hidden causes.
