* Versions: revised since the conference version
* Conference version (36d156218)

*If* you want the agent to plan --- to score whole pattern cascades as policies, not single steps, and to prefer an action whose payoff is two or three ticks out over a greedy one-step win. *However* a rollout that looks one step ahead demonstrates the machinery but not depth; without a temporal hierarchy the horizon stays shallow. *Then* score the whole cascade over a horizon of more than one step, and introduce a two-timescale hierarchy: a slow (strategic) loop that updates priors and preferences, and a fast (tactical) loop that acts against them. Scoring a route means adding up the costs of its steps with the later ones discounted, so that a distant payoff can matter without swamping the immediate situation. *Because* active inference plans over a /policy/ --- here, the pattern cascade for the selected mission --- not over the next action alone. /Invariant to preserve:/ the thing being scored is the cascade, not its first move.
