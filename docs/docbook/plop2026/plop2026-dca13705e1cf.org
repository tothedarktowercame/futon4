* Versions: revised since the conference version
* Conference version (36d156218)

*If* you want a mission's build phase (instantiate) to close only when its selected pattern cascade has actually been /constructed/ --- "a live instance", not a rich prose section describing one. *However* the honest-looking witness is a tautology in disguise: re-run the same fold that proposed the wiring and check it reproduced itself (a mirror, R16), or --- the failure we hit --- execute the cascade as a strict linear /pipeline/ through a fixed rule table, which silently abstains on every mission whose patterns that table does not already encode, so a genuinely well-posed cascade reads as unbuildable. *Then* take seriously that a cascade is a /semilattice, not a chain/ --- patterns compose both by sequence (one enables the next) and by overlap (one pattern belonging to several sub-constructions at once), and in practice the overlaps outnumber the sequences --- and execute it by an /independent/ rollout over that structure --- give each pattern a move interface whose /consumes/ are the salient tokens of its *If*/*However* clause and whose /produces/ are the tokens of its *Then*; admit a move only when it chains onto the frontier the earlier moves have built; and read discharge as the fraction of the mission's want-signature the assembled construction covers.[fn:1] Mint the typed witness (=<mission>.executed.edn=) only when the construction both /assembles/ (at least one box was built) and /discharges/ (it covers some part of what the mission wanted), recording how much it covered as the realised signal; otherwise the phase re-opens. *Because* the rollout reconstructs the wiring from the patterns' /own text/ --- a different computation from the phylogeny-edge fold that predicted it --- so realised \(\ne\) expected by construction: the witness is external to the predictor and cannot be farmed, which is precisely what R16 demands of a closed loop and what the strict-pipeline and re-run-the-fold alternatives both fail to give.

[fn:1] The want-coverage used here is a token-level /proxy/ for true discharge; training the move-prior on a structural-quality label (which cascades historically went well) is its natural strengthening, and turns the same rollout into a first estimate of the policy value R13 asks for. The executor is (over the =cascade_rollout= move interface); the witness it earns is read by (=instantiate-witness?=).
