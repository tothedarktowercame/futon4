* Conference version (36d156218)

*If* you want belief updates to be principled, tunable (one learning rate; a separate trust setting per evidence channel), and operationally robust. *However* without an explicit rule the agent either slams its beliefs to each new observation (over-trusts) or never moves them (over-anchors), and an uncertainty estimate with no floor under it decays toward false certainty. *Then* move each belief /toward/ what was actually observed, but only as far as the learning rate and the trust placed in that channel allow; and track how uncertain the belief is by keeping a running average of the size of recent misses, measured against the freshly-updated belief, never letting that uncertainty fall below a floor. It is the /size/ of a miss that counts, not its direction --- an overshoot and an undershoot are equally informative about how well the channel is understood --- and the floor is a standing admission that no channel is a perfect instrument. *Because* moving in proportion to trusted error is what predictive coding means, and the floor keeps uncertainty in a range where the ambiguity half of the action score still says something. /Invariant to preserve:/ the belief must be able to move, and its uncertainty must not be able to reach zero.

* Current version (87895d15c)

*If* you want belief updates to be principled, tunable (one learning rate; a separate trust setting per evidence channel), and operationally robust. *However* without an explicit rule the agent either slams its beliefs to each new observation (over-trusts) or never moves them (over-anchors), and an uncertainty estimate with no floor under it decays toward false certainty. *Then* move each belief /toward/ what was actually observed, but only as far as the learning rate and the trust placed in that channel allow; and track how uncertain the belief is by keeping a running average of the size of recent misses, measured against the freshly-updated belief, never letting that uncertainty fall below a floor. It is the /size/ of a miss that counts, not its direction --- an overshoot and an undershoot are equally informative about how well the channel is understood --- and the floor is a standing admission that no channel is a perfect instrument. *Because* moving in proportion to trusted error is what predictive coding means, and the floor keeps uncertainty in a range where the ambiguity half of the action score still says something. /Invariant to preserve:/ the belief must be able to move, and its uncertainty must not be able to reach zero. /Choice in this implementation:/ the War Machine keeps its belief as a probability over discrete states (which tokens of work are established) rather than as per-channel averages, and there are three standard ways to update such a belief. The textbook scheme [cite:@parr2022active , eq. 4.13] scores each state against the /expected logarithm/ of the transition probabilities from the previous belief; it scales to large models, but when the previous belief is uncertain and a transition is certain it can rule out every state at once and refuse an observation that was in fact possible. The usual engineering remedy adds a small floor to the transition probabilities, which removes the refusal by making every step a deliberate approximation. We use the third: exact Bayesian updating, which multiplies how likely the observation is under each state by the probability the previous belief gives that state, then renormalises. Here the state spaces are small enough for that to be cheap; it is the belief that minimises the same free energy exactly; it refuses only an observation of probability zero; and it gives the same belief as the textbook scheme whenever the previous state is known exactly.
