* Conference version (36d156218)

*If* you want belief updates to be principled, tunable (one learning rate; a separate trust setting per evidence channel), and operationally robust. *However* without an explicit rule the agent either slams its beliefs to each new observation (over-trusts) or never moves them (over-anchors), and an uncertainty estimate with no floor under it decays toward false certainty. *Then* move each belief /toward/ what was actually observed, but only as far as the learning rate and the trust placed in that channel allow; and track how uncertain the belief is by keeping a running average of the size of recent misses, measured against the freshly-updated belief, never letting that uncertainty fall below a floor. It is the /size/ of a miss that counts, not its direction --- an overshoot and an undershoot are equally informative about how well the channel is understood --- and the floor is a standing admission that no channel is a perfect instrument. *Because* moving in proportion to trusted error is what predictive coding means, and the floor keeps uncertainty in a range where the ambiguity half of the action score still says something. /Invariant to preserve:/ the belief must be able to move, and its uncertainty must not be able to reach zero.

* Current version (6c9693ffd)

*If* you want belief updates to be principled, tunable (one learning rate; a separate trust setting per evidence channel), and operationally robust. *However* without an explicit rule the agent either slams its beliefs to each new observation (over-trusts) or never moves them (over-anchors), and an uncertainty estimate with no floor under it decays toward false certainty. *Then* move each belief /toward/ what was actually observed, but only as far as the learning rate and the trust placed in that channel allow; and track how uncertain the belief is by keeping a running average of the size of recent misses, measured against the freshly-updated belief, never letting that uncertainty fall below a floor. It is the /size/ of a miss that counts, not its direction --- an overshoot and an undershoot are equally informative about how well the channel is understood --- and the floor is a standing admission that no channel is a perfect instrument. *Because* moving in proportion to trusted error is what predictive coding means, and the floor keeps uncertainty in a range where the ambiguity half of the action score still says something. /Invariant to preserve:/ the belief must be able to move, and its uncertainty must not be able to reach zero. /Choice in this implementation:/ the War Machine keeps its belief as a probability over discrete states (which tokens of work are established) rather than as per-channel averages, and there are three standard ways to update such a belief. The textbook scheme [cite:@parr2022active , eq. 4.13] scores each state against the /expected logarithm/ of the transition probabilities from the previous belief; it scales to large models, but when the previous belief is uncertain and a transition is certain it can rule out every state at once and refuse an observation that was in fact possible. The usual engineering remedy adds a small floor to the transition probabilities, which removes the refusal by making every step a deliberate approximation. We use the third: exact Bayesian updating, which multiplies how likely the observation is under each state by the probability the previous belief gives that state, then renormalises. Here the state spaces are small enough for that to be cheap; it is the belief that minimises the same free energy exactly; it refuses only an observation of probability zero; and it gives the same belief as the textbook scheme whenever the previous state is known exactly. With likelihood precision in AIF, the tempered model is A_kappa(o|s) = A(o|s)^kappa / sum_o' A(o'|s)^kappa; omitting the per-state normaliser changes the posterior whenever those sums differ across states and kappa != 1. No belief is moved toward an observation by a learning rate. The War Machine's belief is a categorical posterior (R1), and update-step multiplies the likelihood of the observation under each state by the predicted prior and renormalises, which is the 'exact Bayesian updating' the current text describes. One caveat on 'exact': the likelihood is tempered, A(o|s) raised to kappa(w) = log2(1 + w), where w is the observation's weight. That is exact Bayes only when w = 1; other weights are the per-channel trust the conference text asked for, applied as tempering rather than as a step size. Precision-weighted prediction errors do exist for the continuous channels (compute-prediction-error: error times 1/max(variance, epsilon), with typed :present/:absent/:refused records), but they feed scoring and precision, not a moving belief mean.

Close, with differences of detail. Each channel keeps a bounded window of recent prediction errors; its uncertainty is a regularised mean squared error (a variance prior plus the squared errors, centred on zero so a constant miss still counts as imprecision), so 'size' is squared size rather than absolute size. The floor is there twice: variance cannot fall below min-variance, and the resulting precision is clamped between a floor (0.1) and a cap. Whether the errors are measured against the freshly updated belief or the prediction was not settled for this annotation.
