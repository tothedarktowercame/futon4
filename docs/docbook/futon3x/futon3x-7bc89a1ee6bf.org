* What it is
A packaged, reusable declaration of two boundaries in the operator-turn
interpretation loop: what a *turn record* is, and how its *interpretation is
dispatched*.

=futon3c:packages/turn-seam/=

| File | What it is |
|------+------------|
| =turn-record.schema.json= | what a turn record is, independent of who writes and who reads it |
| =dispatch-contract.md= | how interpretation is delegated, and what fire-and-forget owes you |
| =conformance.py= | run against your own records; no dependencies, no host paths |
| =example/= | one real record, passing unmodified |

* The claim
The schema was derived from 159 real records and checked against all of them,
not designed in advance. If an independent implementation produces records
that pass =conformance.py=, the two agree about what a turn is, and either can
be replaced without the other being edited.

* The mistake it was built after
The convention for quoted material was first designed as /strip the quoted
text/ --- a verb belonging to one consumer, the interpreter. An hour later the
display needed that text back, and a side-channel had to be retrofitted to
carry what had already been discarded.

The generalisable rule: *a seam fails when you design it around what one
consumer needs rather than around what the thing is.* "Strip the quote" is a
consumer's verb. "A turn has a said part and a quoted part" is what the thing
is, and it serves both readers without privileging either.

* Three things that bite an implementer
1. *Offsets* are codepoints, zero-based, end-exclusive. A JavaScript client
   counts UTF-16 units and will disagree silently on any emoji or CJK.
2. *Two texts, not one.* A quoting turn carries both the elided form and what
   was typed.
3. *Delivered is not done.* A dispatch returning cleanly means the brief
   arrived, not that the work happened --- record the job id and ask later.
   152 records accumulated in an indeterminate state before anything noticed.
